### Characters (`src/player/bone-map.ts`, `retarget.ts`, `figure.ts`)

- The player is the generated man, 1.78 m tall. Pedestrians alternate between the man and the woman (1.68 m).
- **Bone map by shape.** Bones are mapped by shape, not by name, so a future generated character needs no new table:
  - Toes are the two lowest leaf bones.
  - Hands are the bones with four or more children. The thumb is the finger root nearest the wrist; index to pinky are sorted front to back.
  - Arms are the chain up from each hand. The spine is the chain from the chest down to the legs' parent.
  - Facing comes from the toe direction.
  - `bone-map.test.ts` loads all three glbs in Node. It checks the map against the hand-made tables in AssetHub's `INTEGRATION.md`, including the swapped "Left/Right" names, and checks that the mannequin maps onto itself.
- **Retargeting.** Each mapped bone takes the mannequin bone's world rotation, offset by the difference between the two rest poses.
  - Each rig bone is first turned to point along the mannequin bone, which turns the A-pose arms into T-pose ones.
  - The hips' travel is scaled by leg length and applied to the bone that carries the legs (Tripo's root).
  - Clips are baked on first use and cached, so all 46 UAL clips are available while only the ones played are ever built.
- **Gun socket.** A `GunHand` child of the right hand turns exactly like the mannequin's `DEF-handR`, so the pistol grip offsets carried over unchanged.
- **Head fix.** The woman has no neck bone, and her rig skinned parts of the face to the shoulder bones, which tore the face when the arms moved. On load, every vertex above the shoulders within 14 cm of the spine is bound to the chest.
- **Poses checked side by side with the mannequin:** idle, walk, sprint, jump, swim, drive, death, talk, and pistol aim. Limbs are not twisted and feet are on the ground. See the `rig-*` screenshots.
- **Clothes.** Pedestrians' clothes are dyed in five hues by a shader (`src/life/outfit.ts`):
  - The shader leaves skin tones, whites, grays, and blacks alone.
  - Nobody in the crowd wears the player's teal shirt.
- Target dummies stay crash-test mannequins.
- If a generated character fails to load, that slot falls back to the mannequin.

### Cars (`src/vehicle/car-model.ts`, `car-specs.ts`, `car-paint.ts`, `wheel-model.ts`)

- Parked cars, the player's car, and 6 of the 10 traffic cars are the generated sports car. The other 4 traffic cars (every third one, counting the first) are the police sedan.
- **Sizing.** Each body is 1 unit long in the glb. `car-specs.ts` holds what was measured from the vertex profiles:
  - Size, and the wheel-arch centers and heights.
  - Real length: 4.6 m for the sports car, 5.0 m for the police car.
  - Narrowing: the generated bodies come out about 18% too wide for their length, so both are narrowed by 0.85.
  - Clearance, and the driver's seat.
  - Physics uses these numbers, so a car drives the same before and after its body loads.
- **Wheels.** Our own procedural wheels: a lathed tire with rounded shoulders and a five-spoke alloy, about 900 triangles each.
  - They sit in the arches, spin, and steer as before.
  - The rims face outward on both sides.
- **Body shader** (one `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with clear coat per car, all sharing one compiled program):
  - Repaints the baked texture's colorful pixels into six paints: orange, red, turquoise, pink, silver, and black.
  - Finds the lamps by color and position in model space:
    - White pixels at the nose are headlights.
    - Deep red pixels at the tail are brake lights.
    - Red and blue pixels above the roof line form the police light bar.
  - Lights each lamp from a per-car `lamps` vector. There are no extra lamp meshes or lights.
- The police light bar alternates red and blue three times a second while the wanted level is above zero.
- Draw calls per car fell from about 25 (CarConcept) to 9.

### Palms (`src/world/vegetation.ts`, `instancing.ts`)

- The generated palm replaces the flat procedural palms, instanced.
- Palms are grouped into 100 m cells, each a `THREE.LOD`. Cells whose center is more than 110 m away draw the 2.3k-triangle palm instead.
- The procedural palms remain only as the fallback if the glb fails.

## Known gaps

- People:
  - The woman's rig has no neck, so her head turns with her chest. Her tank-top strap tears slightly at the shoulder in arm-heavy poses (aim, sprint), visible only up close.
  - Lying dead, the woman floats a few centimeters above the ground.
  - Only two body types, so faces repeat. The dyed clothes hide this at a distance.
- Driving:
  - The driver is placed by a hand-tuned seat offset.
  - The generated bodies have opaque baked glass, so the driver is hardly visible. A sit-down pose that matches the seat exactly was not tuned.
- Lamps:
  - Lamps are found by color, so a few stray texture pixels of the same color light up too: a red badge near the tail, or white trim at the nose.
  - The police headlights are dark in their texture and barely glow.
- Police:
  - The light bar is emissive only. It adds no real point lights, because adding lights recompiles every shader.
  - Police cars are a patrol, not a pursuit; nothing chases or shoots back yet.
- Wheels:
  - The sports car's paint variants come from a hue turn, so its black trim, glass, and grilles stay as baked.
  - All four wheels are the same width, and one rim design is used for every car.
- Palms:
  - There is one palm model. Variety comes only from height and turn.
  - A palm between about 40 m and 180 m away can draw at either detail, depending on its cell.
- No paid generation was used. Everything here is the owner's existing AssetHub output.
